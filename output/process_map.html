The question
Is rush hour expensive because more cars use the same road, or because the road itself behaves differently at rush hour?
Same curvePeak hours just sit further along one speed–density curve. Price by how crowded the road is.
Shifting curveThe curve itself moves with the clock. Price by time of day.

EXIT2

Look before using it

Exploratory checks on the raw data. Two findings shaped everything after.

86% of station-hours are not real readings. When a sensor breaks, PeMS fills in an estimate from its neighbours. Those estimates can't tell us how traffic behaves, so they have to go.
Working sensors are lopsided. Almost half sit on I-10 and I-210, while crashes cluster on I-5, I-405 and I-110. Working sensors fall from 267 an hour in January to 140 in September.

EXIT4

Build the instrument

A simple comparison of speed and traffic mixes up cause and effect, because rain or a crash changes both. We need cars that arrive on our road for a reason unrelated to our road: drivers fleeing an incident on a nearby parallel freeway.

Freeway links1,137 interchange-to-interchange stretches, located from ramp sensors.
Incident matchingEach incident to the nearest sensor within 1 mile, same freeway and direction: 94% matched.
Coverage159 of 422 sensors have a parallel freeway; 1.3% of hours are treated.
Our shortcut. Bento et al. used Google Maps routes for real commutes. We used nearby parallel freeways instead, to finish in time. Say so in the write-up.

EXIT6

Turn it into tolls

Bento et al.'s road model: speed is flat to the kink, then falls 3.8% per extra car; past peak flow, cars queue. Time is valued at $24.30 per vehicle-hour, and drivers cut trips when charged (elasticity −2.0, with a range). For 9,931 station × hour cases we find the toll that maximises welfare under four policies.

IdealA separate toll for every station and hour.
HourlyOne toll per hour, the same everywhere.
Two-hourOne toll per two-hour window.
FlatOne toll all day, everywhere.

Answer: same curve

Rush hour is expensive because the road is crowded, not because of the hour. A toll that follows real-time crowding would do what a time-of-day schedule does, and much more.
