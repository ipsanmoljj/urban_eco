## Main findings

**1. Only 10% of mainline station-hours have real readings.** Of 12.6M mainline station-hours, 10.8M (86%) are fully imputed by PeMS, 0.56M are partly observed, and 1.23M (9.8%) are fully observed. Imputed data can't be used for the slope: imputed speeds bunch at a few values (Figure 3), because PeMS fills them from neighbouring stations and history.

**2. The fully observed data is clean.** No missing values, top speed 82.9 mph, and only 51 rows with lane flow above 2,500 vehicles per hour (max 2,952). That's much cleaner than Bento et al.'s 2017 sample, whose maximum lane flow of 5,173 is physically impossible.

**3. Our sample is less congested than Bento et al.'s.**

| Measure | D7 2026, observed | Bento et al. 2017, Table 1 |
|---|---|---|
| Station-hours | 1,227,191 | 1,362,961 |
| Stations | 430 | 1,318 |
| Lane speed, mean (mph) | 61.2 | 60.9 |
| Lane flow, mean (veh/hr) | 882 | 1,438 |
| Lane density, mean (veh/mile) | 16.7 | 28.2 |
| Hours above the 21 veh/mile kink | 29.0% | not reported |
| Hours above 30.6 veh/mile (flow peak) | 11.0% | not reported |

Mean density is 40% lower than theirs. Some of that is because the surviving sensors sit on less congested roads, and some may be real change since 2017. Either way, the above-kink sample, which drives the main result, is about 356,000 station-hours.

**4. The speed–density curve has the expected shape** (Figure 1). Speed holds near 65–68 mph at low density and starts falling around 20 vehicles per mile, close to Bento et al.'s kink of 21. Flow per lane peaks near 1,800 at 25–35 vehicles per mile, then falls. The plot shows several parallel bands, meaning different stations follow different curves. Station fixed effects are essential.

**5. Rush hour shows up clearly** (Figure 2). Weekday median speed falls from about 68 mph at night to about 59 mph from 9 am to 5 pm. At 5 pm, 65% of station-hours are above the kink.

**6. Working sensors and crashes are on different freeways** (Figure 4). I-5, I-405 and I-110 carry 34% of D7 collisions but only 9% of usable sensor hours. I-10 and I-210 hold 47% of sensor hours but 19% of collisions. I-105 has 4% of collisions and almost no working sensors. This doesn't break the instrument, because a crash on an alternative route only needs a location, not a working sensor. It does mean the roads we can measure are not the roads where most crashes happen.

**7. The station panel is unbalanced** (Figure 5). 152 of 430 stations report in all nine months. 89 stations reported only in January and February and then stopped, mostly on SR-118 and SR-23 in Ventura County. That explains most of the drop in coverage between January and March.

**8. Lane counts.** For 98.9% of observed rows, the number of lanes reporting matches the metadata. In 12,952 rows (1.1%), fewer lanes report than the metadata lists.

**9. Station metadata is stable.** Between July and October, 8 mainline stations were removed, none were added, and no station moved. Every station in the Jan–Sep data appears in the metadata. Using the July metadata for January to June is therefore low-risk, though the earlier versions are still worth getting.

**10. Crash records are complete.** Every D7 collision has a freeway, direction, postmile and coordinates. There are 131 collisions a day, and 377 incidents a day of all types (traffic hazards are the largest group). Bento et al. report 471 "crashes" a day for 2017, which is closer to our all-incident count than to our collision count. Their definition of a crash needs checking in the Dataverse package.

## Proposed cleaning rules (need confirmation)

| # | Rule | Rows affected | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Keep mainline (ML) rows only | drops HOV, ramps, etc. | Keep, as in Bento et al. |
| 2 | Keep `% Observed = 100` only | keeps 1,227,191 | Keep. Lowering to 75% adds few rows and brings in imputed lanes. |
| 3 | Drop rows where fewer lanes report than the metadata lists | 12,952 (1.1%) | Drop. Per-lane density is unreliable when a lane is missing. |
| 4 | Drop rows with zero flow but a recorded speed | 2,380 (0.2%) | Drop. Median speed in these rows is 68 mph, which looks like a detector fault, not empty roads. |
| 5 | Drop lane flow above 2,500 veh/hr | 51 | Drop, as in the brief. |
| 6 | Drop 4 stations whose speed barely varies (sd < 1 mph) | 2,714 | Drop. Stuck detectors. |
| 7 | Keep speeds below 5 mph | 104 | Keep. These are plausible jams. |
| 8 | Density = flow ÷ lanes reporting ÷ speed | all | Use lanes reporting, which equals metadata lanes once rule 3 is applied. |
| 9 | Main sample: all 430 stations. Robustness check: the 152 stations present in all nine months | — | As stated. |
| 10 | Crash definition: CHP codes 1179/1181/1182/1183 (collisions) plus 20001/20002 (hit-and-run) | 40,646 D7 events | Main definition. All incidents as a robustness check, after checking Bento et al.'s definition. |
| 11 | Keep holidays, with a holiday flag | — | Keep, so they can be dropped later if needed. |
